Parent Prompt Pack

15 prompts that help your child learn without doing the work for them · Fred Noseit (aiedume.ai) · Free

The golden rule: ask the AI to coach, not to give answers. Sit with younger children while they use it.

Homework help (hints, not answers)

  1. My child is in grade [x] and stuck on this question: [paste]. Give one hint at a time. Don't give the answer.
  2. Explain [topic] to a [age]-year-old using an example about [their interest, like soccer or dinosaurs].
  3. Check this answer and say if it's right. If not, give a hint about where the mistake is: [paste].
  4. Make 5 practice problems like this one, a little easier: [paste]. Put the answers at the end so I can hide them.
  5. Ask my child 3 questions to see if they really understand [topic].

Reading & writing

  1. Suggest 5 books for a [age]-year-old who likes [interest]. Give the reading level for each.
  2. Ask 5 fun questions about the book [title] to talk about at bedtime.
  3. My child wrote this. Give 2 things they did well and 1 thing to improve, in kind words: [paste].
  4. Make a spelling game with these words: [list].

Everyday learning

  1. Plan a 20-minute kitchen science experiment for a [age]-year-old using things at home. Include safety steps.
  2. Make a weekend scavenger hunt that practises [skill: counting, reading, shapes].
  3. Create a simple weekly chore and reward chart for a [age]-year-old.
  4. Give me 5 conversation starters to talk with my teen about using AI honestly at school.
  5. Make a 2-week study plan for my child's test on [topic], 20 minutes a day.
  6. Explain what [app/game] is and what parents should know about its safety settings.

Before you start

  • Check the app's age rules. Many need users to be 13+ or have a parent's OK.
  • Ask the teacher what AI use is allowed for homework.
  • Keep it short and mix in screen-free time.

AI can be wrong. Check facts before you use them with students or children. Never type a child's full name, address, school, photo or grades into an AI tool.
