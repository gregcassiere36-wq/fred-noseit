🧠 AI-Smart Lessons & Free Websites

Short lesson ideas for PreK-5th, 6th-8th and 9th-12th teachers and for parents. Each one is free and safe. Never type student names, grades or health details into a chatbot.

🧸 PreK-5th: What is AI?

  1. Sort like a computer (20 min). Give pairs 12 animal picture cards. They invent a rule ("has stripes") and sort. Say: AI learns rules from lots of examples, and it can guess wrong.
  2. Draw for the computer. Play Quick, Draw! on the big screen. Ask: how did it guess? When did it get it wrong?
  3. Translate a note home. Prompt: “Rewrite this class note in [language] at a simple reading level. Keep dates and times exact.” Have a speaker check it if you can.
  4. Adapt for ELL and IEP. Prompt: “Make 3 versions of this task: with pictures, with shorter steps, and as a read-aloud script. Grade [ ].” No names or diagnoses.

🧪 6th-8th: Spot fakes and use AI honestly

  1. Spot the fake (40 min). Show 4 posts (2 real, 2 AI-made). Teach 3 questions: Who made it? What's the proof? Who else says so? Exit ticket: judge one new post.
  2. Class AI agreement. Prompt: “Write a one-page class agreement for grade [ ] about when AI is a helper (ideas, feedback) and when it is cheating (handing in its words). Use kid-friendly words.”
  3. Train a simple AI. In Teachable Machine students record 2 objects, test, then try to fool it. Discuss: why did it make mistakes? (Bias from too few examples.)

🎓 9th-12th: Check facts, give fair feedback, explore careers

  1. Fact-check the chatbot (45 min). Students ask a chatbot 5 questions on the unit, then check each claim against 2 real sources and cite them. Score: claims that held up.
  2. Fair essay feedback. Prompt: “Using this rubric, give 3 strengths and 3 next steps for this essay. Don't give a grade.” Remove the name first; you edit and you grade.
  3. Career interview. Prompt: “Pretend you're a [job]. A student will interview you; answer in plain words.” Then verify pay and training on the Occupational Outlook Handbook.

👪 Parents: fakes, translation and screen time

  1. Talk about fakes. Look at one viral post together and ask the 3 questions. Remind your child: voices and videos can be faked too.
  2. Translate school notes. Prompt: “Translate this school note into [language]. Keep dates, times and names of places exact.” Then double-check the dates.
  3. Set screen-time limits. Use Google Family Link (Android) or Apple Screen Time (in iPhone Settings). Agree on the limit together.

🌐 Free trusted websites

  • Code.org AI lessons: free AI and coding lessons, K–12.
  • Day of AI: free AI literacy lessons by grade, from MIT RAISE.
  • Common Sense Education: free digital citizenship and AI lessons.
  • ScratchJr (ages 5–7) and Scratch (ages 8+): free coding.
  • Khan Academy Kids: free learning app for ages 2–8.
  • Experience AI: free AI lessons for ages 11–14.
  • Checkology: free news-literacy lessons for spotting misinformation.
  • Common Sense Media: age ratings for apps, games and AI tools.
